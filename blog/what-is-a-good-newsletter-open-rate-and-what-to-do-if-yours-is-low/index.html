What Is a Good Newsletter Open Rate (And What to Do If Yours Is Low)

June 7, 2026 · Top of Funnel

Founders ask this question as if there is one universal benchmark that applies to every newsletter in every industry.

There is not. But there are useful reference points, and more importantly, there is a more useful question you should be asking instead.

The benchmarks worth knowing

Average email open rates across industries sit between 20 and 30 percent for B2C email and 15 to 25 percent for B2B.

For founder newsletters — personal, editorial newsletters sent to opted-in subscribers — the benchmarks tend to be higher. Well-run founder newsletters typically see 40 to 60 percent open rates on established lists, and some highly engaged niche audiences see even higher.

If your newsletter is sitting below 20 percent, something is wrong. Either the list has gone cold from inconsistent sending, the content is not delivering on what subscribers expected, or there is a deliverability issue pushing emails to spam or promotions.

If you are between 30 and 45 percent, you are in a solid range. If you are above 45 percent consistently, your audience is highly engaged.

Why open rate is the wrong primary metric

Apple's Mail Privacy Protection, introduced in 2021, changed open rate measurement permanently. When a reader uses Apple Mail with privacy protection enabled, the email registers as opened even if the person never looked at it. This inflates open rates for most senders by a meaningful amount.

This does not mean open rate is useless. It means it should not be the only signal you track.

The metrics that matter more for founder newsletters are reply rate, click rate, and most importantly, the downstream business results: how many subscribers have become enquiries, how many enquiries have become clients.

A newsletter with a 35 percent open rate that generates three client enquiries per month is performing better than a newsletter with a 55 percent open rate that generates none. Open rate is an input. Revenue impact is the output.

What actually drives open rate for founder newsletters

Three things move the needle more than anything else.

List hygiene. A list full of inactive or disengaged subscribers drags your open rate down. Cleaning your list regularly, removing subscribers who have not opened in six months, and re-engagement campaigns for borderline subscribers all improve your measured open rate and your actual deliverability.

Sending consistency. Lists that go dark for weeks or months and then suddenly receive an email perform worse than lists that receive regular weekly communication. Subscribers build habits around newsletters they trust to show up. Break that habit and you lose opens.

Subject line quality. This is the most direct lever. A better subject line gets more opens. Over time, as subscribers learn that your subject lines consistently deliver value, they will open before even reading the subject line — because your name in the sender field is enough signal. That name recognition only develops through consistency.

How to improve a low open rate

If you are below 25 percent and looking to improve, start with the list before touching the content.

Segment out subscribers who have not opened in 90 days and send them a re-engagement email. Something direct: "Are you still interested in hearing from me? Here is what we have been covering." Give them a reason to stay. Those who do not open the re-engagement email should be removed from your active list.

Next, look at your sending domain. If you are using a free email service or a subdomain that has not been authenticated with proper SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records, a portion of your emails are likely landing in spam. Fix the authentication before doing anything else.

Then look at your subject lines over the last ten issues. Are they specific, curiosity-generating, and written for one person? Or are they generic and descriptive? Improving subject lines is the fastest way to see an open rate lift within a few issues.

The open rate that actually matters

The most important open rate is not your overall list average. It is the open rate among your most recent subscribers.

New subscribers who just joined should be opening at the highest rates if the welcome experience is good and the first few issues deliver on expectations. If your newest subscribers are already disengaged, the problem is upstream — either the welcome sequence is not working, or the newsletter is not living up to what they signed up for.

Fix the experience for new subscribers first. Everything else improves from there.
