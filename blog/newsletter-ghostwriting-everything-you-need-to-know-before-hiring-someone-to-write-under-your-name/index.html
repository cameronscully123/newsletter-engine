Newsletter Ghostwriting: Everything You Need to Know Before Hiring Someone to Write Under Your Name

May 23, 2026 · Bottom of Funnel

What it is, how it works, and what to watch out for before you sign anything

Ghostwriting is one of the oldest arrangements in business. Executives have had speechwriters for decades. Authors have had collaborators for longer. CEOs have had comms teams writing under their name since long before the internet existed.

Newsletter ghostwriting is the same thing applied to email — and it's quietly become one of the smartest moves a time-poor founder can make.

Here's everything you need to know before hiring someone to write yours.

What Is Newsletter Ghostwriting?

Newsletter ghostwriting is when someone writes your email newsletter for you, in your voice, and it goes out under your name. Your subscribers have no idea someone else wrote it. As far as they're concerned, you sat down and wrote every word yourself.

The ghostwriter's job is to sound exactly like you — your tone, your way of framing ideas, your opinions. A good one will write sentences you'd be proud to have written yourself. A bad one will make your audience quietly wonder why you suddenly sound like a LinkedIn post from 2019.

How Does It Actually Work?

The process varies by who you hire, but a solid newsletter ghostwriting engagement typically looks like this:

Voice capture first. Before any writing happens, the ghostwriter does a deep dive into how you communicate. They'll read your old content, listen to interviews or recordings, ask you questions about your opinions, your clients, your business. Some do formal voice documents. Others just get on a call and take notes. Either way, this is the most important step — and if someone skips it, that's a red flag.

A brief or input from you each week. Most ghostwriters need a starting point. That might be a 10-minute voice note, a topic you want to cover, a few bullet points, or even just a "write about X this week." The less input required from you, the more experienced the ghostwriter.

A draft lands in your inbox. You read it, tweak anything that doesn't sound right, and approve it. Some founders barely touch drafts. Others have a back-and-forth. After a few issues, most founders find they're changing less and less as the ghostwriter locks in the voice.

It goes out under your name. That's it. Your audience gets a newsletter that sounds like you, thinks like you, and builds trust with them on your behalf. Every week, without you carving out 3–4 hours to make it happen.

What Newsletter Ghostwriting Is Not

It's worth being clear about the boundaries, because the term gets used loosely.

Newsletter ghostwriting is not the same as a done-for-you newsletter service. A ghostwriter delivers content. You still manage the platform, set up the send, handle your list, and figure out your own growth. If you want the whole operation handed off, you need a full newsletter management service — not just a writer.

It's not content marketing. Your newsletter isn't a blog post optimised for SEO. It's a direct relationship channel. A ghostwriter who comes from a content background and treats your newsletter like a blog will produce something that technically counts as a newsletter but doesn't actually build trust or drive revenue.

It's not AI with a human name on it. There are a lot of cheap services selling "ghostwritten" newsletters that are just AI-generated content with some light editing. Your audience can feel the difference. So can anyone who's read your actual writing.

What to Look For When Hiring a Newsletter Ghostwriter

1. Do they ask about your voice before pitching deliverables?

If someone's first conversation with you is about word counts and turnaround times before they've asked a single question about how you think or what you care about, walk away. Voice capture is everything. It's the foundation of every issue that follows.

2. Can they show you samples in different voices?

A ghostwriter who can only write in one style isn't really capturing voice — they're just writing in their own voice and slapping your name on it. Ask to see work they've done for different clients with different tones.

3. Do they understand email, or just writing?

A great copywriter and a great newsletter ghostwriter are not the same person. Newsletter writing has its own rhythm. Subject lines, hooks, open loops, the way you build a point across 600 words and land a CTA without it feeling forced. Look for someone who actually understands how email readers behave.

4. How much input do they need from you?

The whole point of hiring a ghostwriter is to get time back. If they need 45 minutes of your time per issue to brief them, you'd be better off writing it yourself. The best ghostwriters need a topic direction and a quick voice note — nothing more.

The Trade-off to Be Aware Of

Newsletter ghostwriting gives you consistency and saves you time. What it doesn't give you is full operational control without any effort on your part.

You still own the list management, the platform, the growth strategy. If your subscriber count stagnates, that's not your ghostwriter's problem. If your welcome sequence is broken, they won't know. If your deliverability tanks, they're not the ones to fix it.

That's why many founders who start with a ghostwriter eventually move to a full done-for-you newsletter service — because they want the whole thing handled, not just the writing.

Is Newsletter Ghostwriting Worth It?

If your newsletter exists but it's inconsistent, a ghostwriter fixes that.

If your newsletter doesn't exist because you never have time, a ghostwriter fixes that too.

Email is still the most valuable direct channel a founder has. A list of even 1,000 engaged subscribers is worth more to your business than 10,000 social followers you don't own. The founders who figure this out early and find a way to publish consistently — whether they write it themselves or not — are the ones building real leverage in their business.

The ones still waiting for a free Tuesday afternoon to finally write that issue aren't.
