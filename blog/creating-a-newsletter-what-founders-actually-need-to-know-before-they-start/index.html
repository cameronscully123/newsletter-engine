Creating a Newsletter: What Founders Actually Need to Know Before They Start

June 8, 2026 · Top of Funnel

Everyone says you should start a newsletter.

Few people tell you what that actually involves, where most founders go wrong, or what separates the newsletters that compound into real business assets from the ones that quietly get abandoned after seven issues.

Here is the honest version.

What you are actually building

A newsletter is not a content channel. It is a relationship at scale.

The founders who get the most out of their newsletter understand this from the start. They are not trying to produce content. They are trying to build a direct, ongoing relationship with the people most likely to become their clients, refer their clients, or amplify their work.

Once you see it that way, almost every question about what to write, how often to send, and what to measure becomes easier to answer. You ask: what would strengthen this relationship? What would a valuable, trusted person say to someone they are trying to help?

The ones who think of it as a content channel tend to optimise for the wrong things — volume, variety, polish — and end up with something that reads like it was produced rather than written.

The decisions that actually matter before you start

Who exactly are you writing to? Not your target market. One person. A specific type of person with a specific situation, at a specific point in their journey. The more clearly you can describe that person, the better every issue will be.

What will every issue leave them with? Not a topic area. A specific type of value. Something they will be able to think, do, or decide better than they could before they read it. If you cannot answer this clearly for your first ten imagined issues, the newsletter does not have a clear enough premise yet.

What will you write about when you have nothing to say? Because that week will come. Probably in week three or week nine or week fourteen. The founders who stay consistent are the ones who have a reliable system for generating ideas before they sit down to write, not the ones who wait for inspiration.

The part most guides skip

Creating a newsletter that works is not primarily a writing challenge. It is a consistency challenge.

The value of a newsletter compounds over time. The first issue builds almost nothing. The fifteenth issue is when subscribers start talking about it. The fiftieth issue is when it starts generating meaningful business results. The hundredth issue is when it becomes a genuine competitive asset.

This means the critical factor is not how good your first issue is. It is whether you are still sending in month twelve.

The founders who stay consistent are almost always the ones who have taken the execution off their plate. Either they have built a non-negotiable habit around writing time and protect it ruthlessly, or they have a system or team that keeps the newsletter going regardless of what else is happening in the business.

The ones who stop are typically the ones who treat the newsletter as something they do when they have capacity. When capacity disappears, the newsletter disappears with it.

What to focus on in the first 90 days

In the first three months, the only thing that matters is sending consistently and learning what your audience responds to.

Do not optimise the design. Do not spend three weeks deciding on a name. Do not wait until the welcome sequence is perfect before you launch. The most important thing you can do in the first 90 days is send twelve issues without stopping.

By issue twelve, you will have real data: which subject lines get opened, which topics get replies, which sections get forwarded. That data is worth more than any pre-launch planning.

Growth in the first 90 days comes almost entirely from your personal network and consistent social promotion around each send. Tell people you are building something. Share what you are learning. Make it easy to subscribe and give people a specific reason to do so right now rather than someday.

The minimum viable newsletter

You do not need a complicated setup to start. A plain-text email from a reliable platform, a clear one-sentence description of who it is for and what it covers, a simple subscribe page, and a commitment to send every week.

That is the whole system. Everything else, the design, the automations, the segments, the monetisation — is an upgrade you add once the core habit is established and you have real subscribers to learn from.

The founders who overthink the setup almost always start later than they should and send less consistently once they do. The ones who start simple and iterate from real feedback almost always end up with something better.
