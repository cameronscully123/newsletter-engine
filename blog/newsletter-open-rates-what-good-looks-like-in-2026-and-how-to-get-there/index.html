Newsletter Open Rates: What Good Looks Like in 2026 (And How to Get There)

June 10, 2026 · Top of Funnel

Everybody obsesses over open rates. They are the first number most founders check after a send, the metric that gets screenshotted and shared, the figure that feels like proof your newsletter is working.

The problem is, most founders are benchmarking themselves against the wrong targets or optimising for the wrong thing entirely.

Here is what open rates actually mean in 2026, and what to focus on instead.

What the Numbers Actually Look Like

The honest benchmark for a B2B newsletter in 2026 is an open rate somewhere between 30% and 45%, depending on list size, platform, and how long subscribers have been on your list.

If you are below 25%, your content, subject lines, or list hygiene needs attention. If you are above 45%, you have built something genuinely engaged. Most founders publishing consistently to a warm, opted-in audience sit comfortably in the 35% to 45% range.

Consumer newsletters tend to track lower. B2B newsletters, where readers have opted in with a professional purpose, consistently outperform the average.

Why Your Dashboard Numbers Are Not Reliable

Since Apple's Mail Privacy Protection rolled out, open rate data has become significantly less reliable. Apple Mail pre-loads email tracking pixels, which logs an "open" whether or not the reader actually viewed your email.

If a large proportion of your list uses Apple Mail, your reported open rate is inflated. Not dramatically, but enough to make raw open rate comparisons misleading.

This does not mean open rates are useless. They are still a directional indicator of list health and subject line performance. It means you should treat them as relative signals, comparing your own issues to each other, rather than holding them against industry averages as if they are precise measurements.

What Actually Drives Opens

Open rates live and die on two things: your subject line and your sender reputation.

Sender reputation is the less glamorous factor. If your emails end up in spam or promotions folders, open rates tank regardless of how good your subject line is. Consistent sending, clean list hygiene, a verified sending domain, and avoiding spam-trigger language all contribute to staying in the primary inbox.

Subject lines are the thing you can actively test and improve. The highest-performing subject lines tend to be short (under 50 characters), specific, and either curiosity-led or value-explicit. A subject line like "Why your open rates dropped" outperforms "Newsletter Issue 47" by a wide margin.

Numbers work well. "3 things" or "5 reasons" signal a defined, digestible structure. Questions create engagement. First names in the subject line still lift opens modestly. Emojis, used selectively, add visibility in a crowded inbox.

What does not work: vague teasers, generic labels, excessive punctuation, and anything that looks like it came from a marketing automation sequence.

The Metric That Matters More Than Opens

Click-through rate is a far stronger indicator of whether your newsletter is actually working.

A 40% open rate means nothing if nobody clicks through to your content, your offers, or your calls to action. A 30% open rate with a 6% click rate is a newsletter with a genuinely engaged audience.

For founders using their newsletter to generate leads and clients, click-through rate tells you how many people took action, not just how many had the email delivered to their inbox.

Track your click rate. Compare it across issues. Notice which topics and formats drive the most clicks. That is the data that tells you what your audience actually cares about.

How to Improve Your Open Rate

If your open rates are lower than you want, the fix is usually one of a few things.

First, clean your list. Subscribers who have not opened anything in six months are dragging down your deliverability and inflating your inactive base. Remove them or run a re-engagement campaign first.

Second, audit your subject lines. If they are generic, vague, or newsletter-labelled, rewrite them as headlines. Specific beats clever. Value beats intrigue. Short beats long.

Third, check your sending time. For a B2B audience, Tuesday to Thursday mornings tend to outperform Monday and Friday sends. That said, consistency matters more than the specific day.

Finally, look at your first paragraph. Many email clients preview the first line of body copy alongside the subject line. A strong opening line adds a second hook that drives the decision to open.

Consistency Is the Compound Lever

Open rates improve naturally when you publish consistently. Readers who see your name regularly in their inbox develop a habit of opening. The founders with the highest open rates are almost always the ones who have never missed a send in two years.

That is a consistency problem as much as it is a strategy problem. If staying consistent is what is holding you back, that is the thing worth solving first.
