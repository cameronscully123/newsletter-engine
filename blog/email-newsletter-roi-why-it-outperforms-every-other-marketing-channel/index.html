Email Newsletter ROI: Why It Outperforms Every Other Marketing Channel

June 5, 2026 · Mid Funnel

Every marketing channel promises results. Most of them cannot prove it.

Email newsletters are the exception. The data on newsletter ROI is consistent, has been consistent for years, and keeps improving as every other channel gets noisier and more expensive.

If you are a founder trying to decide where to invest your content and relationship-building time, here is what the numbers say.

The return on email is not close to anything else

Email marketing generates between 36 and 40 dollars for every dollar spent. That is a 3,600 to 4,000 percent return on investment, and it is the figure most cited across industry studies on channel performance.

For context: paid social typically returns 2 to 4 dollars per dollar spent. SEO is harder to attribute but generally produces lower short-term returns with longer payback periods. Events and sponsorships are even harder to measure and typically underperform on a cost-per-revenue basis.

No other owned marketing channel produces ROI at the level email consistently does. The gap between email and everything else is not marginal. It is structural.

Why email outperforms on ROI

The reason email produces outsized returns is not complicated once you understand what it actually is.

Email is a direct line to someone who asked to hear from you. Every subscriber opted in. There is no algorithm deciding whether your message gets seen, no ad spend required to reach your own audience, and no platform that can deprioritise your content without warning.

When you send a newsletter to 2,000 subscribers, roughly 15 to 20 percent open it in the first 24 hours. That is 300 to 400 people actively reading what you wrote. Compare that to posting on LinkedIn, where organic reach on a post to 2,000 followers might be 50 to 150 views, most of which are passive scrolls rather than engaged reads.

The intent and attention level of an email reader versus a social media scroller is fundamentally different. That difference is why the conversion rates are so different.

The B2B case is even stronger

For founders operating in B2B markets, where trust and relationships drive buying decisions, newsletters are particularly powerful.

Eighty-one percent of B2B marketers report that email newsletters are their most used content marketing method. That is not because B2B marketers are slow to adopt new channels. It is because newsletters work in B2B in a way that social media rarely does.

B2B buyers make considered decisions. They research extensively before committing. They want to understand how a potential partner or vendor thinks before they get on a call. A newsletter that arrives weekly, demonstrates deep expertise, and stays relevant to the buyer's situation does exactly what the B2B sales process requires: it builds informed trust over time.

The sales cycles that close fastest are almost always the ones where the buyer already knew the seller well before the first conversation. A newsletter accelerates this systematically and at scale.

What founders typically underestimate about newsletter ROI

The headline ROI figures are compelling, but they understate the actual value for founders in a few important ways.

The compounding effect is not captured in one-year ROI figures. A newsletter list you build this year is an asset you own indefinitely. Each subscriber you add represents not just one potential conversion but years of future touchpoints, referrals they might make, and relationships they might broker. The ROI of email compounds in a way that paid advertising does not.

Referral value is typically not measured. The clients who came through a newsletter referral, where a subscriber forwarded your issue to someone who then reached out, are almost never attributed to the newsletter in standard analytics. The actual reach and influence of a newsletter extends beyond the direct subscriber list.

The trust differential shortens sales cycles. A lead that came through your newsletter already trusts you. They read your thinking every week. The sales conversation is shorter, the objection handling is easier, and the close rate is higher. This effect is real and material but almost impossible to capture in a simple ROI calculation.

The cost side of the equation

The ROI figures above assume you factor in the true cost of running a newsletter, including your time.

For a founder writing their own newsletter, the honest time cost is three to four hours per week. At a typical founder hourly rate, that is a meaningful expense that is rarely included in ROI calculations but should be.

When the fully-loaded cost is included, the ROI picture shifts depending on how you value your time. For founders billing 200 to 400 pounds per hour, the time cost of writing a newsletter is 600 to 1,600 pounds per month. A done-for-you newsletter service at a fraction of that cost starts to look not just convenient but economically sensible.

The newsletter ROI is real. The question is whether you are capturing it yourself or leaving it on the table because execution keeps getting deprioritised.
