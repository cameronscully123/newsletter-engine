Email Marketing for Agencies: Why Most Agencies Are Doing It Wrong

May 26, 2026 · Mid Funnel

The agencies getting the best results from email aren't doing more. They're doing it differently.

Most agencies approach email marketing the same way they approach everything else they do for clients: as a production problem.

Write content. Send it. Repeat. Track open rates. Adjust subject lines. Send again.

That's not email marketing. That's email scheduling. And it explains why so many agency newsletters exist but almost none of them actually generate business.

Here's what the agencies getting results are doing differently.

The Fundamental Mistake: Confusing Activity With Strategy

Sending a newsletter consistently is not the same as having an email marketing strategy.

The agencies with active newsletters but no results are usually making one of two mistakes.

The first is writing content that's interesting to them but irrelevant to the people they want to hire them. Industry news, company updates, case study announcements, award recognitions. Content that keeps the agency top of mind in the most forgettable way possible.

The second mistake is having no clear objective behind the content. Every issue goes out without a clear answer to the question: what should a reader think, feel, or do after reading this that they weren't doing before?

Email without strategy is just noise in someone's inbox. And inboxes are already full of noise.

What Email Marketing Should Actually Do for an Agency

An agency's email list is one of the most valuable assets it has, and most agencies are drastically underusing it.

Done properly, email does three things for an agency:

It keeps warm leads warm. B2B sales cycles are long. A prospect who wasn't ready six months ago might be ready now. The agency that's been in their inbox consistently is the one they call first. The agency that went quiet is the one they've forgotten.

It builds trust before the first conversation. The best agency new business conversations start with the prospect already believing the agency knows what it's doing. A year of consistent, specific, useful email content does that work. By the time someone reaches out, they're not evaluating whether the agency is credible. They already know it is.

It generates referrals from people who aren't even clients. Subscribers who find an agency's newsletter genuinely useful tell other people about it. Not because they're asked to, but because good content is worth sharing. Referrals from subscribers who've never spent a penny with the agency are more common than most people expect.

The Content Mistake That Kills Agency Newsletters

The biggest reason agency newsletters fail to generate business is that they write about what the agency does instead of what the client needs to know.

There's a version of every agency newsletter that reads like a brochure. Service announcements. Process explanations. Thought leadership that's really just an extended pitch.

And there's another version that reads like it was written by someone who genuinely understands the problems facing the people they work with, and has something real to say about how to think about those problems.

The first version gets archived. The second version builds a pipeline.

The shift is simple to describe and genuinely hard to execute: stop writing about your agency and start writing for your ideal client. Every issue should serve the reader first. The business development benefits follow from that, not the other way around.

The Growth Problem Most Agencies Don't Address

Writing great content is only half of it. The other half is making sure the right people are actually on the list.

Most agency email lists are a mix of past clients, current clients, a few prospects, some industry contacts, and a long tail of people who signed up years ago and haven't engaged since.

That list is not the same as an audience. It's a contact database with an unsubscribe button.

Building an actual email audience for an agency means actively driving the right people to the list. That means publishing the newsletter content as blog posts that can be found via search. It means promoting content through LinkedIn to reach the kind of clients the agency wants to attract. It means thinking about list growth as an ongoing priority, not an afterthought.

The agencies that treat their email list as a strategic asset are the ones building something that compounds. Every subscriber who stays engaged raises the value of the list. Every piece of content that earns a new subscriber makes the next one easier to get.
