LinkedIn Newsletter vs Email Newsletter: What Every Founder Needs to Know

June 21, 2026 · Top of Funnel

LinkedIn newsletters are everywhere right now. Founders, consultants, and agency owners are spinning them up at pace, pointing to open rates of 40 to 50 percent as proof they're working. On the surface, it looks like a no-brainer.

But the comparison between a LinkedIn newsletter and an email newsletter is not really about which one gets more opens. It's about what you're actually building, and whether any of it belongs to you.

The answer matters more than most founders realise.

What LinkedIn Newsletters Do Well

LinkedIn gives you two things that take years to build on a standalone email platform: built-in distribution and built-in discovery.

When you publish your first LinkedIn newsletter, the platform notifies your connections and followers automatically. That means you can reach thousands of people from day one without running ads, building a landing page, or convincing anyone to hand over their email address. For founders who are early in their content journey, that initial momentum feels meaningful.

LinkedIn newsletters also rank in Google search. A well-optimised LinkedIn newsletter article can pull in organic traffic from people who have never heard of you. That is a real advantage that most email platforms simply cannot offer.

The Problem With Renting Your Audience

Here is the part that gets overlooked: you do not own a single LinkedIn newsletter subscriber.

You cannot export the list. You cannot email those subscribers directly through another platform. You cannot contact them if LinkedIn changes its algorithm, removes newsletters, or simply makes the product less prominent. You have no way to reach them outside of LinkedIn itself.

That is not a mailing list. It is a following. And there is a significant difference.

Followings are fragile. A single platform decision, an account restriction, or a product sunset can erase years of audience-building overnight. Founders who have experienced this on Twitter, Facebook, or Instagram will recognise the feeling. You built something real, and then you did not have it any more.

What an Email Newsletter Gives You That LinkedIn Never Will

When someone subscribes to your email newsletter, their address is yours. You can move platforms, change tools, switch providers, and bring your entire audience with you. No permission required. No platform intermediary.

Email also gives you a direct relationship that LinkedIn's algorithm can interrupt. On LinkedIn, your newsletter lands in someone's notification feed alongside everything else. In their inbox, it arrives as a direct communication between you and them.

That relationship compounds over time. A reader who has been receiving your email newsletter for 18 months knows your voice, trusts your perspective, and is far more likely to become a client or a referral source than someone who passively follows your LinkedIn updates. Email builds depth in a way that social platforms cannot replicate.

Email newsletters also give you data that LinkedIn withholds. You can see who opens every issue, who clicks specific links, who has gone quiet, and who engages consistently. That behavioural data helps you understand your audience and improve what you send.

The Right Way to Think About LinkedIn Newsletters

LinkedIn newsletters are useful. They are just not useful as your primary newsletter.

Think of LinkedIn as a top-of-funnel discovery tool. Someone finds your newsletter through a search result or a notification. They read an issue. If it is good, they are interested. The goal at that point is to convert them from a LinkedIn subscriber to an email subscriber, where you actually own the relationship.

This means every LinkedIn newsletter issue should include a clear call to action pointing readers to your email sign-up page. You are using LinkedIn's distribution to fill your own funnel, not to build an audience that lives on LinkedIn's servers.

That distinction matters more as your newsletter grows. A list of 5,000 email subscribers that you own is more valuable than 20,000 LinkedIn newsletter followers that you do not.

The Algorithm Problem

LinkedIn posts and articles are subject to reach limitations that email is not. Organic reach on LinkedIn has declined steadily over the past few years, and newsletters are not immune to those forces.

Your LinkedIn newsletter may show a high open rate today. But those numbers are partly a function of novelty. As more founders publish LinkedIn newsletters, the notification system becomes noisier, open rates compress, and the algorithm decides which newsletters to surface more prominently. You have no control over any of that.

Your email open rate reflects the real relationship you have with your list. It goes up when you improve your writing and down when you lose relevance. It is not managed by an algorithm.

Building Something That Lasts

The founders who are most consistent in their content marketing understand a simple principle: build on land you own.

Social platforms, LinkedIn included, are rented space. They are valuable for growing your audience and reaching new people. But the list itself, the direct access, the relationship data, all of that needs to live somewhere you control.

An email newsletter is the asset. A LinkedIn newsletter is the amplifier. Use them in that order, and both become more powerful.

If you are publishing on LinkedIn and not converting those readers to your own email list, you are doing the hard work of content creation without building anything that belongs to you. That is the trade-off worth understanding before you decide where to focus your energy.
