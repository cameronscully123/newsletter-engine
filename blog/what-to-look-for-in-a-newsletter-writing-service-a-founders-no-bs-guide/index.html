What to Look For in a Newsletter Writing Service (A Founder's No-BS Guide)

May 24, 2026 · Bottom of Funnel

How to tell the difference between a service that builds your business and one that just fills your send schedule

Most founders who look for a newsletter writing service aren't looking for a writer.

They're looking for someone who can take the whole thing off their plate, make it sound like them, and actually move the needle for their business. They just don't always know how to say that when they start searching.

Here's what to actually evaluate before you hire anyone.

First, Get Clear on What You Need

Newsletter writing services exist on a spectrum. Before comparing options, know which end you actually need.

Writing only: Someone produces the copy. You handle everything else. Platform, list, sending, growth. This is ghostwriting, not full service.

Writing plus sending: The copy gets written and the send gets set up. You still own the strategy, the list management, and the growth.

Full newsletter management: Strategy, writing, sending, list management, and growth all handled. You review a draft once a week and approve it. That's your involvement.

Most founders who say they want a "newsletter writing service" actually want the third option. They just don't realise that's what they're describing until they hire the first option and still feel like they're doing all the work.

Know which one you need before any sales call.

What to Look For: The Non-Negotiables

1. They capture your voice before writing a single word

This is the most important thing on this list and the easiest to skip over.

Any service can write competent newsletters. Very few can write newsletters that actually sound like you. The difference is in how much work they do before the first issue goes out.

Before agreeing to write anything, a good service will want to understand how you communicate. They'll ask about your opinions, your clients, your way of seeing your industry. They'll study your existing content. Some do formal voice documents. Others take detailed notes from a call.

If a service sends you a questionnaire with five generic questions and calls that "voice capture," that's your answer.

2. They ask what your newsletter is supposed to do for your business

The best newsletter writing services aren't just thinking about content. They're thinking about outcomes.

What happens after someone reads your newsletter? Are they more likely to reach out? Are they warming up to eventually buy? Are they sharing it with people who should know you exist?

A service that doesn't ask these questions is going to write newsletters that fill your send schedule without doing anything for your pipeline. Content for content's sake.

The question to ask them: "How do you make sure the newsletter actually supports our business goals?" Their answer will tell you everything.

3. They have samples in multiple voices

This one is simple but important. Ask to see examples of newsletters they've written for different clients.

If every sample sounds similar, they're not capturing voice. They're writing in their own style and putting different names on it. That newsletter might be good. It just won't sound like you.

What you're looking for is range. One sample that's punchy and direct. Another that's more considered and analytical. Another that's warm and conversational. If they can show you that, they can probably find your voice too.

4. They're transparent about what's included

Newsletter writing services often look similar until you read the contract.

Get specific answers to these before you commit:

  • Who writes it? Is it always the same person or does it rotate through a team?
  • What happens if you don't like the draft? How many revisions are included?
  • Do they handle the platform and sending, or just the content?
  • What do they need from you each week, and how much of your time does that take?
  • What happens to your list and your platform access if you leave?

The last one matters more than most founders think. Your email list is your asset. Make sure any service you use understands that and has clear answers about data ownership.

5. They understand email, not just writing

Good writing and good email writing are different skills.

Email has its own rhythm. Subject lines have to earn the open before anyone reads a word. The first sentence has to justify the time it takes to keep reading. The structure needs to hold attention through to a call to action that doesn't feel forced.

A content writer who's great at blog posts or social media isn't automatically good at newsletters. Ask how they approach subject lines. Ask how they think about open rates. Ask what they'd do if your open rate started dropping.

If they can't answer those questions confidently, they're a writer, not a newsletter service.

The Question Most Founders Forget to Ask

Before signing with anyone, ask: "What does success look like after 90 days?"

A service worth hiring will have a real answer. They'll talk about the voice being locked in by issue three or four. They'll mention open rate benchmarks. They'll ask about your business goals so they can connect the newsletter output to something measurable.

A service that can't answer will shrug and say something about "quality content." That's not a plan.

The Real Test

The best newsletter writing services are ones you'd be happy for your audience to never know about.

The newsletter goes out. It sounds like you. Your subscribers never wonder who wrote it. Your clients assume you're just one of those founders who's disciplined enough to publish every week.

That invisibility is the proof that the service is doing its job properly.
