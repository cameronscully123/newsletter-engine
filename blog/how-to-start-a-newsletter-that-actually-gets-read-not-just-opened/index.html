How to Start a Newsletter That Actually Gets Read (Not Just Opened)

Starting a newsletter is easy. Most platforms will have you set up and ready to send in under an hour.

The harder problem is starting a newsletter that people actually read when it arrives. Not one that gets opened out of habit and closed before the second paragraph. One that people look forward to, finish, and come back for.

Here is what separates the newsletters people read from the ones they just open.

The open is not the goal

Most newsletter advice optimises for open rates. The subject line, the send time, the preview text.

Those things matter. But they are not the goal. They get the email opened. What keeps people reading is entirely different.

A newsletter that gets opened and not read is a newsletter people are politely ignoring. They subscribed once, they open it occasionally out of guilt or habit, and they will unsubscribe the next time they do an inbox clean. Getting unsubscribed from is not just a list hygiene issue. It is a signal that the newsletter is not delivering what it promised.

The goal is not opens. The goal is readers who finish the issue and are already looking forward to next week's.

Start with a single clear promise

Before you write your first issue, write one sentence that describes exactly what your newsletter is and who it is for.

Not "insights for entrepreneurs." Not "tips and strategies for growth." Something specific: what type of person gets the most value from this newsletter, and what specific problem does it help them with?

The clearer that promise, the easier every subsequent decision becomes. What to write about, what to leave out, how to grow the list, how to measure whether it is working.

Most newsletters that do not get read failed at this step. They were started without a clear enough point of view about who they are serving. The content ends up broad and generic because there was no specific reader in mind.

The welcome email is the most important thing you will write

The first email a new subscriber receives sets the entire relationship.

Most welcome emails are wasted. They say something like "Thanks for subscribing! Here is what to expect." Then the subscriber waits for the next weekly issue, which arrives days later when they have forgotten why they signed up.

A welcome email that works does something different. It immediately delivers on the reason the person subscribed. It gives them something useful right now, not a vague promise of future value. It tells them exactly what they will get and how often. And it asks them a question, so they reply and your emails land in their primary inbox instead of promotions.

Write the welcome email before you write the first issue. It is what turns a subscriber into a reader from the very first interaction.

Write like you are talking to one person

The most common reason newsletters do not get read is that they sound like newsletters.

Formal. Polished. Written for an audience rather than a person. That tone creates distance. Readers feel like they are receiving content, not a message from someone who understands their situation.

The newsletters people actually read sound like they were written specifically for the person reading them. Not because the writer personalised it technically, but because they were so specific about who they were writing for that each reader feels personally addressed.

Pick one person. A specific past client, a specific friend in your industry, a specific type of person you most want to reach. Write every issue directly to that person. Do not try to be useful to everyone. Be indispensable to one person and everyone who matches that profile will feel the same way.

Make finishing easy

People stop reading newsletters when reading them feels like work.

Long paragraphs. Dense text. No clear structure. Ideas that meander without landing anywhere.

Short paragraphs are easier to read. Clear headings help readers navigate. One main idea per issue makes it easy to understand what you are saying. A strong ending that lands the point makes finishing satisfying.

The best newsletters are easy to read in five minutes and leave the reader with something they did not know before or a perspective they had not considered. That is the experience worth building.
