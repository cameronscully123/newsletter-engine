Newsletter Ideas for Founders: 25 Topics Your Audience Actually Wants to Read

The hardest part of running a newsletter is not the writing. It is deciding what to write about every single week without running out of ideas or repeating yourself.

Most founders approach this wrong. They sit down on newsletter day, stare at a blank page, and try to think of something interesting. That is a recipe for generic content and eventual burnout.

The founders who publish consistently and never seem to run dry have a different approach. They have a bank of reliable topic categories they pull from — so picking what to write each week is a five-minute decision, not a two-hour struggle.

Here are 25 newsletter topic ideas across five categories that work for any founder audience.

Category 1: Lessons from your own experience (5 ideas)

These are the most powerful topics you can write about. Nobody else has your specific experience. Nobody else can write these in the same way.

  1. A mistake you made in your business that cost you time, money, or clients — and what you learned from it.
  2. A decision that felt risky at the time but turned out to be the best thing you did.
  3. Something you believed about your industry three years ago that you no longer believe.
  4. A client situation that changed how you approach your work.
  5. The thing you wish you had known when you started, written as advice to yourself five years ago.

Category 2: Counterintuitive takes (5 ideas)

These topics perform well because they challenge assumptions. They make the reader stop and think.

  1. The common advice in your space that you think is wrong — and why.
  2. Something your ideal client is spending money on that probably is not working as well as they think.
  3. A metric everyone in your industry tracks that you think is mostly meaningless.
  4. The thing people avoid doing because it seems hard, that is actually simpler than it looks.
  5. Why the "obvious" solution to a common problem in your space often makes it worse.

Category 3: Behind the scenes of your business (5 ideas)

Transparency builds trust faster than almost anything else. Readers are curious about how things actually work.

  1. How you price your work and what goes into that decision.
  2. How you find clients and what your business development actually looks like week to week.
  3. A process or system you use that saves you significant time.
  4. How you decide which clients to take on and which to turn down.
  5. What a typical week looks like for you — the reality, not the polished version.

Category 4: Help your reader think through a decision (5 ideas)

Decision-support content is valuable because it is immediately useful. Readers keep coming back to newsletters that make them better at their work.

  1. A framework for making a decision your audience faces regularly.
  2. How to know when a strategy is not working versus when it just needs more time.
  3. The questions to ask before making a specific type of hire, investment, or commitment.
  4. How to evaluate whether a vendor, agency, or tool is actually doing a good job.
  5. When to stay the course versus when to change direction — and how to tell the difference.

Category 5: Observations about your industry (5 ideas)

These topics position you as someone who is paying close attention and has informed views. They build authority without requiring you to share personal information.

  1. A trend you are watching and what you think it means for your audience's business.
  2. Something that is changing in your space faster than most people realise.
  3. A problem that keeps coming up in conversations with clients or peers — and what it says about the state of the industry.
  4. A comparison between how things were done three to five years ago and how they are done now, and why the shift happened.
  5. Something you keep seeing done well (or badly) across the businesses in your space, with specific observations about what separates the two.

How to use this list

Do not try to plan too far ahead. Pick your topic for next week from whichever category feels most natural right now. Over time, you will find that certain categories feel easier to write and produce better responses from your readers. Build more of those into your rotation.

The goal is not to cover every topic. It is to publish something worth reading every week without grinding to a halt. Use this list as a reference when you are stuck, not as a content calendar to work through in order.
