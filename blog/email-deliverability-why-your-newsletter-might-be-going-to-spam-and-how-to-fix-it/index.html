Email Deliverability: Why Your Newsletter Might Be Going to Spam (And How to Fix It)

June 8, 2026 · Top of Funnel

You spend an hour writing a newsletter. You hit send. And half your subscribers never see it.

Not because they unsubscribed. Not because they stopped caring. Because it landed in spam.

This is the deliverability problem, and it is more common than most founders realise. The good news is that it is entirely fixable once you understand what is actually happening.

What Email Deliverability Actually Means

Deliverability is not just whether your email was technically sent. It is whether your email reached the primary inbox, the promotions tab, or the spam folder of each individual subscriber.

An email can be sent successfully without ever being seen. Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail all use filtering systems that assess your email before deciding where to place it. Most senders assume they are landing in the inbox. Many are not.

The industry average inbox placement rate sits around 83%. High-performing senders consistently hit 90% or above. That gap matters more than you think. If you have 5,000 subscribers and a 17% spam rate, almost 850 people never see your email at all.

Why This Happens

Inbox providers use a combination of technical signals and engagement data to make their filtering decisions.

The technical signals come first. If your sending domain does not have SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records configured correctly, you are already at a disadvantage. These are the authentication protocols that prove to Gmail and Outlook that you are who you say you are. Without them, your email looks suspicious before a single person has read it.

Beyond authentication, inbox providers watch how people actually behave when they receive your emails. High open rates signal that your audience wants to hear from you. Low open rates, high delete rates, and spam complaints signal the opposite. Over time, these signals build your sender reputation, which is the invisible score that follows your domain everywhere.

The List Hygiene Problem

One of the fastest ways to destroy your sender reputation is sending to people who do not want to hear from you.

This sounds obvious, but it happens constantly. Old lists with outdated email addresses generate hard bounces. Subscribers who stopped engaging months ago drag down your open rate. People who meant to unsubscribe but did not hit spam instead.

Your bounce rate should stay below 2%. Your spam complaint rate should stay below 0.1%. These are not aspirational targets. They are the thresholds that inbox providers use when deciding whether to trust you.

Cleaning your list regularly is not a sign of weakness. It is how you protect the subscribers who actually want to read what you send.

How to Fix It

Start with authentication. Make sure your sending domain has SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all configured correctly. Most newsletter platforms will walk you through this setup. If yours has not prompted you to do it, do it now.

Next, warm up your domain before scaling. If you are sending to a new list or dramatically increasing your volume, do it gradually. Start with your most engaged subscribers. Let the inbox providers see positive engagement signals before you expand. A sudden spike in volume from a domain with no history looks like spam even if it is not.

Purge disengaged subscribers on a regular basis. Anyone who has not opened an email in six months is a liability to your reputation. Remove them, or run a re-engagement campaign first. If they do not respond to that, let them go.

Write subject lines that get opened. Deliverability and engagement are directly connected. The more people open and read your emails, the better your sender reputation becomes over time. A great subject line is not just good marketing. It is infrastructure.

The Platform You Use Matters Too

Not all newsletter platforms handle deliverability equally. Some manage their shared sending infrastructure well. Others allow bad actors onto their platform, which damages the reputation of everyone sending from the same IP range.

Dedicated IPs, domain-level authentication, and active spam monitoring are worth checking when you evaluate platforms. If your platform is lax about who it allows on, your reputation can suffer because of someone else's behaviour.

Most founders do not think about this until they already have a problem. Check it before you do.

Why Founders Ignore This (And Why It Costs Them)

Most founders treat their newsletter like a blog. Write it, publish it, move on.

The difference is that a blog post either loads or it does not. A newsletter either reaches the inbox or it does not, and you often have no idea which is happening.

Open rates alone will not tell you the full story. If your emails are going to spam, the people who do check spam are not counted in your open rate either. You may have a bigger problem than your metrics suggest.

Running a basic deliverability test takes ten minutes and can surface issues that have been quietly throttling your performance for months.

A newsletter is only as valuable as the number of people who actually read it. Fix the foundation before you focus on the content.
