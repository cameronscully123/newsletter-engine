How Often Should You Send a Newsletter? The Founder's Guide to Getting Frequency Right

June 14, 2026 · Top of Funnel

At some point, every founder starting a newsletter asks the same question. How often should I send this?

It feels like a tactical question but it's actually a strategic one. The answer you choose affects your relationship with your audience, the quality of what you produce, and whether your newsletter survives long enough to compound into something valuable.

Here's how to think through it properly.

Why Frequency Matters More Than You Think

Your newsletter frequency isn't just a production schedule. It's a signal. It tells your subscribers what to expect and how to feel about you in their inbox.

Send too rarely and you become a stranger. Subscribers forget they signed up. Your open rates drop because there's no rhythm, no habit, no anticipation. When you do send, it feels random rather than reliable.

Send too often without the content to match and you become noise. People start skimming and then ignoring and eventually they unsubscribe. Not because they don't like you, but because you trained them not to pay attention.

The goal is to send often enough that you stay present, but with enough substance that each issue is worth opening.

The Case for Weekly

Weekly is the most common starting frequency for a reason. Around 65% of newsletter creators send weekly. It gives you seven days to gather ideas, develop a point of view, and write something worth reading. It builds a reliable presence in the inbox without overwhelming your readers or yourself.

For most founders and agency owners, weekly is the right place to start. It's often enough to stay relevant. It's infrequent enough to maintain quality. And it creates the kind of cadence where readers start to look forward to your next issue.

The one exception is if you're building a daily digest or a news-driven newsletter. Some formats require daily sends because the content itself is time-sensitive. But for most founder-led newsletters built around insight, opinion, or expertise, weekly is the default that works.

The Case for Bi-Weekly or Monthly

Some founders can't produce a quality weekly newsletter and run their business at the same time. That's not a failure of ambition. It's an honest assessment of capacity.

If weekly means rushing, publishing filler, or burning out by month three, then bi-weekly or monthly is the better choice. Consistency over time matters far more than frequency in the short run. A monthly newsletter that's been running for three years builds more trust than a weekly one that went quiet after six weeks.

The risk with monthly is visibility. You go from showing up four times a month to showing up once. For a personal brand newsletter or a thought leadership send, once a month can still work if the content is genuinely valuable and your readers know to expect it. But you'll need to work harder to build the habit in your audience.

How to Choose Your Frequency

Start by asking yourself three questions.

First: how much time can you realistically dedicate to writing and editing each week? Be honest about your current bandwidth, not your aspirational bandwidth.

Second: do you have enough ideas to fill every issue without padding? A newsletter that runs out of ideas after eight weeks is more damaging to your brand than not having one. If your ideas come in bursts, bi-weekly may serve you better.

Third: what does your audience actually need? A newsletter for agency owners tracking fast-moving industry shifts benefits from more frequent sends. A newsletter built around long-form thinking on business and strategy can hold its own at monthly.

The Frequency Trap

The most common mistake founders make is starting at a frequency they can't sustain. They launch weekly because it feels professional and committed, grind through the first few months, and then start missing issues. Inconsistency is more damaging than a lower frequency.

Research consistently shows that irregular sending drives unsubscribes more than high frequency does. A reader who expects you every Tuesday and gets nothing for three weeks doesn't trust you the same way after that.

Set a frequency you can keep. Then keep it. Build from there if you want to increase.

You Can Always Change It

Nothing about your newsletter frequency is permanent. If you start bi-weekly and find yourself with more to say, move to weekly. If weekly is draining your best thinking before it can reach clients, drop to bi-weekly with a note to your readers.

Your audience will follow you if you explain the change. A short line in your next issue is enough: "Starting next week, you'll hear from me every Tuesday instead of every other week. I have more to share and I don't want to hold it back."

Most readers appreciate that kind of transparency. It signals that you're taking the newsletter seriously, which is exactly the kind of signal a founder-led newsletter should be sending.

The Real Question Behind the Frequency Question

Behind the question of how often to send is a deeper question: are you going to take this seriously and keep going?

Frequency matters. But commitment matters more. A founder who sends a thoughtful, honest, specific email every week for two years builds something that compounds into real business outcomes. Pipeline. Referrals. Inbound leads. Reputation.

The number in the cadence is less important than the decision to keep showing up.
