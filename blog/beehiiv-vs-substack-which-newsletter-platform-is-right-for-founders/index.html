Beehiiv vs Substack: Which Newsletter Platform Is Right for Founders?

June 5, 2026 · Top of Funnel

Most founders who decide to start a newsletter spend their first hour researching platforms instead of writing their first issue.

Beehiiv or Substack? Kit? Mailchimp? Ghost?

The comparison rabbit hole is real, and it can kill the project before it starts. Here is what actually matters for founders so you can make this decision in five minutes and get on with it.

The actual difference between beehiiv and Substack

The core distinction comes down to one question: do you want to be a writer on a platform, or do you want to run a newsletter as a business asset?

Substack is built around the first model. It is a publishing platform with a built-in reader network. You write, publish, and Substack handles the infrastructure. If you charge readers for access, Substack takes 10% of every dollar you make. That is the trade: simplicity in exchange for a permanent cut of your revenue.

Beehiiv is built around the second model. It is a growth and operations platform — a full newsletter business tool with subscriber analytics, referral programmes, a paid ads network, custom automations, and a web presence. You keep 100% of subscription revenue. You have more control and more complexity.

For the vast majority of founders using a newsletter as a business development tool rather than a standalone media business, beehiiv is the more appropriate choice. The growth infrastructure matters. The revenue cut does not, because the newsletter is generating leads, not direct subscription income.

What beehiiv has that Substack does not

The feature gap between the two platforms is significant and growing.

Beehiiv has a referral programme built in. Subscribers can share your newsletter and be rewarded automatically — this is one of the most effective organic growth mechanisms available and Substack does not offer it natively.

Beehiiv has a Boosts marketplace, where you can pay other newsletters to recommend yours and earn revenue by recommending others. This cross-promotion network is exclusive to beehiiv and is how serious newsletter operators accelerate list growth without relying entirely on organic channels.

Beehiiv has granular subscriber analytics. You can see exactly which subscribers are engaged, which are not, where they came from, and how they behave. That data is essential for improving your newsletter and cleaning your list.

Beehiiv also has a full website builder included, so your newsletter and your web presence are unified on one platform.

Substack has none of these. What it does have is a Substack network — a built-in reader community where Substack users can discover and subscribe to new publications. For writers building a public media brand, this is valuable. For founders building a closed-loop business development tool, it is largely irrelevant.

When Substack makes sense for founders

There are scenarios where Substack is the right choice.

If you want maximum simplicity and the lowest possible barrier to publishing, Substack is easier to get started on. There are fewer decisions to make and fewer settings to configure.

If you plan to charge subscribers for access to your content and want the platform to handle payments with no setup, Substack does this well. The 10% fee hurts at scale but is painless when you are starting out.

If the Substack reader network is relevant to your audience and you want discoverability within that ecosystem, it has genuine value.

For most founders running a B2B newsletter to generate leads and build authority, these advantages are outweighed by what beehiiv offers.

The question that matters more than the platform

Here is the honest truth about the beehiiv vs Substack debate: the platform you choose matters far less than whether you actually publish consistently.

The founders whose newsletters build real audiences and generate real business are not the ones who chose the optimal platform. They are the ones who showed up every week for a year.

A founder who publishes 52 consistent issues on Substack will build more authority and generate more leads than a founder who set up a perfectly optimised beehiiv account and published six issues before getting distracted.

Pick the platform that removes friction for you specifically. If beehiiv's features excite you and the setup does not overwhelm you, use beehiiv. If Substack's simplicity is what gets you to actually write, use Substack. The marginal difference in platform capability is irrelevant if the newsletter does not go out.

What the platform decision cannot solve

Neither beehiiv nor Substack will write your newsletter for you.

Neither will decide what to say each week. Neither will keep you consistent when a client crisis hits and the newsletter gets pushed. Neither will ensure the content is actually compelling enough to build trust with your readers.

The platform is infrastructure. What drives results is the content, the consistency, and the strategy behind it.

Founders who understand this spend less time comparing platforms and more time thinking about what their newsletter is actually going to say and who it is going to say it to.
