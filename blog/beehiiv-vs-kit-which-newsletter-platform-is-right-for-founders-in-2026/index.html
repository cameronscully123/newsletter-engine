Beehiiv vs Kit: Which Newsletter Platform Is Right for Founders in 2026?

June 13, 2026 · Top of Funnel

Picking a newsletter platform is the question founders spend far too long obsessing over.

The honest answer is that the choice matters far less than what you do with it. But since you are going to ask anyway, here is a clear breakdown of the two leading options in 2026 and which one actually fits a founder-run newsletter.

Both beehiiv and Kit (formerly ConvertKit) are solid, well-supported platforms. They are also designed with very different types of users in mind. Choosing the wrong one is not going to sink your newsletter, but choosing the right one will save you time, money, and a fair amount of friction as your list grows.

What beehiiv Is Built For

beehiiv launched in 2022 and became the platform of choice for newsletter-first creators almost immediately. The founding team came from Morning Brew, which means they understood newsletters from the inside, not just as a feature of a broader marketing product.

The platform is built around one central idea: grow your newsletter and make money from it, without needing to stitch together a dozen separate tools. The writing experience is clean and focused. The analytics are genuinely useful. And the growth tools are built into the platform rather than bolted on as an afterthought.

beehiiv includes a referral programme by default, a native ad network that connects you with sponsors, a recommendation system that helps other newsletters send subscribers your way, and a basic website builder included in the plan. For founders who want to focus on the newsletter itself, without getting lost in technical integrations, it is the more intuitive choice.

What Kit Is Built For

Kit has been around since 2013, originally as an email marketing tool for bloggers and course creators. It rebranded from ConvertKit to Kit in late 2024 to reflect its evolution into what the company describes as an email-first operating system for creators.

Where Kit genuinely excels is automation. If you are running complex sequences, selling digital products, tagging subscribers based on their behaviour, or building funnels that connect your email list to courses, memberships, or coaching programmes, Kit handles that complexity better than almost anything else in this space.

The trade-off is cost. Kit raised prices significantly in 2026, and at 10,000 subscribers, you are looking at around $135 per month on the standard paid plan. That is reasonable if the platform is powering your whole creator business. For a founder whose primary goal is a consistent weekly newsletter, it may be considerably more than you need.

Growth Features: beehiiv Has the Better Toolkit

If growing your list is a priority, beehiiv gives you more levers to pull.

The recommendation network means other newsletter operators can send you subscribers directly, with no additional work required from you. The referral programme is built in and easy to activate. The Boosts feature lets you pay for new subscribers within the platform itself, with clear visibility on what you are spending per subscriber.

Kit has a creator network, but it is more limited in scope. For founders who see newsletter growth as a central part of their marketing strategy, beehiiv simply has more to offer on that front.

Automation and Funnels: Kit Wins

For founders who need their email list to do heavy lifting beyond a weekly send, Kit's automation builder is more capable.

You can tag subscribers based on what they click, what they purchase, or how long they have been on your list. You can build sequences that branch based on subscriber behaviour. You can connect Kit to your course platform, your booking system, or your CRM, with emails firing automatically based on what subscribers actually do.

This matters if your newsletter is one piece of a more complex marketing and sales system. It matters much less if your goal is consistent, high-quality weekly content with a relatively simple setup.

Pricing in 2026

beehiiv is the more affordable option for most founders.

The platform has a free plan for up to 2,500 subscribers. The Scale plan, which unlocks most of the growth features, starts at around $39 per month. At 10,000 subscribers, you are typically looking at around $109 per month.

Kit offers a generous free plan for up to 10,000 subscribers, which is one of the better deals in email marketing for founders still in the early stages. But the paid plans are more expensive than beehiiv at most subscriber counts, particularly after the 2026 price increases.

The Question Behind the Question

Founders obsess over platform choice partly because it feels like a decision they can make and then move on from. The newsletter itself is harder. It requires having something worth saying, saying it consistently, and showing up every week even when other things compete for your time.

The platform does not solve that problem.

For most founders and agency owners starting or scaling a newsletter in 2026, beehiiv is the stronger starting point. It is simpler, more affordable, and optimised specifically for newsletters rather than for creator businesses more broadly. Kit is worth it if you are running a more complex digital business with multiple revenue streams and need the automation to hold it together.

Pick one. Start writing. The newsletter matters infinitely more than where you send it from.
